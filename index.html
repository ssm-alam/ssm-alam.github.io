Research Highlights

Selected studies highlighting major research questions, computational approaches and key findings.

RESEARCH HIGHLIGHT 01

PD-L1 Phytochemical Inhibitor Discovery

Structure-guided computational screening was used to investigate naturally derived compounds capable of targeting the PD-L1 immune checkpoint.

Key finding: Hydnocarpin and calomelanol G were identified as promising candidate compounds based on virtual screening, docking and molecular dynamics analyses.
View publication →
RESEARCH HIGHLIGHT 02

PD-1 Peptide & Small-Molecule Discovery

Computational strategies were developed to identify potential inhibitors targeting the PD-1 immune checkpoint.

Key finding: Integrated virtual screening, molecular docking and molecular dynamics approaches identified promising PD-1 inhibitor candidates.
View publications →
RESEARCH HIGHLIGHT 03

PD-L1 Genetic Variations

Computational analysis was used to investigate the effects of non-synonymous mutations in the CD274 gene encoding PD-L1.

Key finding: Multiple potentially deleterious variants were identified, with C40R and V104L showing notable effects on PD-1 interaction.
View publication →
RESEARCH HIGHLIGHT 04

JlpA–Hsp90α Interaction

Functional characterization of the Campylobacter jejuni surface protein JlpA was performed to investigate its interaction with human Hsp90α.

Key finding: The Q332–A354 region of JlpA showed greater importance for Hsp90α binding and host-cell adhesion.
View publication →
RESEARCH HIGHLIGHT 05

SARS-CoV-2 & ACE2 Interaction

Protein–protein interaction approaches were used to investigate interactions between SARS-CoV-2 spike RBDs and ACE2 receptors.

Key finding: Different mutations and variants were associated with altered interaction patterns between viral RBD and host ACE2.
View publications →
RESEARCH HIGHLIGHT 06

EGCG Pharmacological Network

Network pharmacology, pathway analysis, molecular docking and molecular dynamics were integrated to investigate EGCG-associated targets.

Key finding: HSP90AA1 emerged as a prominent common molecular target in the computational analysis.
View publication →
About Me

Research-driven biology

I am a biological sciences researcher with research interests spanning computational biology, cancer immunotherapy, molecular drug discovery, genomics and host–pathogen interactions.

My research integrates computational modelling, molecular docking, molecular dynamics simulations, bioinformatics, genomics and biological experimentation to investigate molecular mechanisms and therapeutic possibilities.

I am particularly interested in combining computational predictions with experimentally testable biological hypotheses.
